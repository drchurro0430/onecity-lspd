# Vehicle Policies

Officers are required to **LOCK THEIR VEHICLE** when they exit their vehicle to avoid vehicle theft and prevent unauthorized access to the equipment available therein. Officers are permitted to escalate to use of lethal force in the event that an individual has stolen their patrol vehicle provided the officer reasonably believes without a reasonable doubt that the individual stealing the vehicle poses an imminent threat of serious injury or death of officers or members of the public. Officers should consider that automatic firearms and other equipment are typically contained and readily available for use within their vehicles and the guidance of Tennessee v. Garner and the states “Fleeing Felon Doctrine” when considering the use of deadly force in this instance should be considered at great length.

Each Officer is issued a “patrol vehicle” based on the rank they hold, all “patrol vehicles” contained in the LSPD Vehicle Structure are implicitly pursuit rated vehicles. The only time an exception can be made to the patrol vehicle structure is for officers active in a subdivision or at the discretion of the office of the Chief or office of the Commissioner. Department Administration reserves the right to revoke access to a patrol vehicle for an officer as a punishment for misconduct or failure to safely operate that vehicle. The vehicles listed in the attached Uniform and Vehicle Structure Worksheet are the only department approved Patrol and Pursuit vehicles, deviation from this list of authorized patrol/pursuit vehicles requires written authorization from the department of the Major or Commissioner. While a pursuit might occur officers will be following the “Los Santos Police Department Vehicular Pursuit Policy”

* Officers are permitted to apply engine, turbo, brake, and transmission upgrades to their vehicles.
* Officers in department vehicles displaying liveries in any fashion must display their badge number on their license plate (26, K26, LSPD26, PD26, etc).
* There is no minimum required number of officers in RTO to patrol in an unmarked vehicle. Per the JDC policy unmarked vehicles must be “earth-tone“ or colors you would normally see in nature.
* Utilization of the lightbar script is prohibited.

<summary><strong>Further Spike Strip Guidance</strong></summary>

The use of spike strips by law enforcement is governed by the following guidelines:

1. Authorization: Spike strips can only be used by personnel who have received proper training. This training is available HERE and in the Probationary Trooper follow on training guide.
2. Risk Assessment: Before deploying a spike strip, law enforcement officers must conduct a risk assessment to determine if its use is appropriate. The following factors must be considered heavily:
   * Traffic volume
   * Weather conditions
   * Nature of the pursuit
   * Location of the spike trap
3. Notification: Law enforcement officers must provide advance notice of a spike strip deployment to other officers involved in a pursuit.
4. Spacing: Pursuing units should fall back and allow a gap between the suspect car and chase cars to allow time for the spike strip to be removed.
5. Placement: Spike strips should be placed in a safe location, away from pedestrian and vehicle traffic. They should also be placed in a manner that allows the targeted vehicle to come to a stop with minimal damage to public or private property.
6. Removal: Once a spike strip has been deployed, it should be removed from the roadway as soon as possible to minimize the risk of injury to civilians and damage to other vehicles. Spike strips should only be used when all other methods of stopping a fleeing vehicle have been exhausted.

## Motorcycles

Pursuit intervention techniques regarding motorcycles involving vehicular contact, application of spike strips or any other maneuver that would alter the course or controllability of the bike itself is seen as application of lethal force. Justification for application of lethal force should be established before any pursuit intervention technique is applied to a 2 wheel motorized vehicle fleeing from law enforcement.
